Company car pool

Our office in Rotterdam shares twelve company cars among about sixty employees who visit customers
and sites. Today reservations go through a shared calendar and a key cabinet, and the fleet
coordinator keeps service dates and damage notes in a spreadsheet. Cars get double-booked, a car
goes out for a customer visit the day its service is due, and damage found on return is noticed
weeks later by the next driver.

We want one system for two jobs.

Reservations. An employee reserves a car for a time slot, stating the trip's purpose and the number
of passengers; the system offers only cars that fit (seats, a van for equipment, an automatic
gearbox when asked) and are free for the slot. A reservation is for a specific car. The employee
picks the car up at the start of the slot and returns it with the mileage read off the dashboard
and a note of any new damage. A reservation not picked up within thirty minutes of its start is
released so others can take the car. An employee may hold at most two future reservations at a
time, each at most three days long; a longer trip needs the fleet coordinator's approval. A car
that is out of service, or whose service is due before the end of the requested slot, is not
offered. Cancellation is free until two hours before the start; later cancellations and no-shows
are counted per employee and the coordinator sees the monthly count.

Fleet care. The fleet coordinator keeps each car's record: plate, seats, gearbox, whether it is a
van, current mileage, next service due by date or by mileage, and the car's state (in service, out
of service). Each return updates the car's mileage; when the mileage or the date crosses the
service threshold, the car becomes service-due and the coordinator books it with the garage and
takes it out of service for the garage days. A damage note on return opens a damage report the
coordinator reviews: minor damage is noted and the car stays in service; damage that makes the car
unsafe takes it out of service at once, which cancels the car's future reservations and notifies
those employees so they can reserve another car. When the garage returns the car, the coordinator
puts it back in service with the new mileage and next service threshold.

Everyone signs in with the company directory. Employees see their own reservations and the no-show
count the coordinator sees. The coordinator is one person today; the office manager covers absences.

Out of scope: fuel cards and expenses, private use of cars, pool cars at other offices, telematics
or keyless entry (keys stay in the cabinet), insurance claims beyond the damage report.
